# MEANINGFRAME — EXECUTIVE PRODUCT BRIEF

## 1. Product in one sentence

MEANINGFRAME guides a Co-Author through a short personal conversation and transforms what makes someone meaningful into a recognisable work of art, a curatorial interpretation and an optional finished Signature Edition.

## 2. Customer problem

People want to honour someone important, but most gifts fail in one of three ways:

- they are generic and say little about the relationship;
- they require creative skills or time the giver does not have;
- “personalisation” means only adding a name or applying a style filter to a photo.

The customer is not primarily looking for AI. They are looking for a gift that says:

> “I see who you are, what shaped you and why you matter.”

## 3. Core product thesis

MEANINGFRAME wins only if it owns the layer between personal information and the final image:

`relationship + memories + identity + symbols + art direction → meaningful artwork`

The image model is replaceable infrastructure. The defensible product is:

- the adaptive Co-Author experience;
- the structured Meaning Map;
- the translation of meaning into visual decisions;
- the pairing of artwork and Curator’s Note;
- the reveal and gifting ritual;
- accumulated quality data about which inputs create valued outcomes.

## 4. Primary personas

### Persona A — The Thoughtful Giver

- Age: typically 35–65
- Need: a significant gift for a parent, partner, mentor, colleague or friend
- Occasion: milestone birthday, retirement, anniversary or gratitude
- Problem: wants the gift to feel deeply considered, not mass-produced
- Buying trigger: sees a preview that recognisably captures the person
- Anxiety: “Will it look generic, strange or cheap?”
- Success: recipient feels seen and the giver is proud to explain the symbols

### Persona B — The Identity Seeker

- Secondary MVP use
- Creates a work for themselves
- Wants an artistic reflection rather than a conventional portrait
- Valuable for non-seasonal demand and social sharing

### Excluded MVP personas

- procurement teams seeking bulk employee awards;
- agencies producing campaigns;
- artists selling work on a marketplace;
- people seeking celebrity deepfakes or direct artist imitation.

## 5. Jobs to be done

### Functional

“Help me create and deliver a personal artwork without requiring artistic or technical expertise.”

### Emotional

“Help me express how I see and value this person.”

### Social

“Help me give something that demonstrates attention, thought and emotional intelligence.”

## 6. Product outputs

Every paid project produces:

1. a confirmed Meaning Map;
2. three refined art variants;
3. one selected and optionally revised final artwork;
4. a Curator’s Note in the selected voice;
5. a digital Reveal Page;
6. a Certificate of Co-Creation and production metadata;
7. print-ready files appropriate to the selected art world.

Signature Edition adds:

8. a 40 × 50 cm Fine Art print;
9. a black or natural frame;
10. a printed Curator’s Note;
11. Swiss standard delivery.

## 7. Product language

Use:

- “Co-Author”, not user or prompt writer;
- “Co-Author Session”, not questionnaire;
- “Meaning Map”, not AI analysis;
- “Art World”, not filter;
- “Curator’s Note”, not AI description;
- “co-created with AI”, not handmade or painted by an artist;
- “one-of-one personal composition”, not unique in a legal or cryptographic sense.

## 8. North-star experience

The decisive moment is not checkout. It is when the Co-Author first sees a preview and says:

> “That is unmistakably them — and I understand why.”

## 9. Quality promise

An acceptable MEANINGFRAME result must:

- preserve recognisable identity where photos are provided;
- visually express at least three confirmed Meaning Map elements;
- avoid invented biographical claims;
- avoid visual artefacts, malformed anatomy and nonsensical text;
- look intentional and compositionally coherent;
- remain faithful to the selected Art World;
- be technically fit for the purchased output.
