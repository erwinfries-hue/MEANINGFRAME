# MEANINGFRAME — MARKET POSITIONING & BUSINESS MODEL

## 1. Market logic

MEANINGFRAME sits at the intersection of:

- personalised gifts;
- custom portraits and wall art;
- AI-assisted co-creation;
- print-on-demand;
- identity expression and emotional storytelling.

The market signal is attractive, but generic AI art is rapidly commoditising. Low generation cost is not a competitive advantage. Meaning, trust, curation, quality assurance and physical presentation are the commercial opportunity.

## 2. Evidence behind the proposition

- Personalised gift markets are forecast to grow, driven by individuality, meaningful connection and online distribution.
- Research published in *Psychology & Marketing* reports that customised gifts can be more appreciated because recipients experience “vicarious pride” in the giver’s participation.
- The opposite risk also exists: presenting AI-generated heartfelt content as entirely one’s own can create perceived dishonesty and guilt. MEANINGFRAME therefore makes the Co-Author’s contribution explicit and discloses AI assistance.

## 3. Competitive frame

| Category | Examples | Strength | Weakness versus MEANINGFRAME |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY AI image tools | Canva, Recraft, general image generators | Fast, inexpensive, flexible | Customer must prompt; no emotional interview, curation, interpretation or gifting ritual |
| Low-cost personalised AI portraits | Etsy sellers and small portrait sites | Cheap and abundant | Mostly photo-to-style; inconsistent quality; weak brand trust; easily copied |
| Human custom portrait services | myDaVinci and commissioned artists | Human craft, credibility | Expensive or slow; often photo reproduction rather than identity interpretation |
| Print-on-demand providers | Gelato, Prodigi, Printful | Scalable production and shipping | Infrastructure, not a differentiated customer proposition |
| Art marketplaces | traditional online galleries | Prestige and discovery | Not meaningfully personalised and not co-created for a recipient |

## 4. Positioning

### Category

Personal identity art / meaningful personalised art.

### Positioning statement

For people who want to honour someone important with more than a conventional gift, MEANINGFRAME is a guided art co-creation experience that turns identity, memories and meaning into a recognisable work of art and a curatorial interpretation. Unlike photo filters or generic AI portrait tools, every result begins with a confirmed Meaning Map and is designed as a finished, dignified gift.

### What MEANINGFRAME is not

- not a prompt playground;
- not a cheap photo filter;
- not a simulated human artist;
- not mass-generated wall decoration;
- not an open marketplace.

## 5. Offer architecture

### Free discovery

- Co-Author Session
- Meaning Map
- one watermarked, protected personalised preview

### Digital Edition — CHF 49

- three refined variants
- one selected final artwork
- one guided revision
- Curator’s Note
- Reveal Page
- Certificate of Co-Creation
- personal-use digital files

### Signature Edition — CHF 229

Includes Digital Edition plus:

- 40 × 50 cm Fine Art print
- black or natural frame
- printed Curator’s Note
- standard Swiss delivery

### Add-ons

- additional guided revision: CHF 19
- Atelier Review: CHF 49

The Atelier Review is a disclosed human review of composition, likeness, artefacts and print readiness. It must not imply a famous curator or artist.

## 6. Preliminary unit economics

The build must store actual costs per order. Do not hard-code assumed margins as facts.

Target guardrails:

| Metric | Digital | Signature |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retail price | CHF 49 | CHF 229 |
| AI + compute target | ≤ CHF 4 | ≤ CHF 5 |
| Payment + service target | ≤ 8% of revenue | ≤ 6% of revenue |
| Production + packaging + delivery target | n/a | ≤ CHF 90 |
| Gross margin before marketing target | ≥ 75% | ≥ 50% |
| Refund/redo reserve | 5% | 8% |

The provider quote and physical samples must validate the Signature margin before public launch.

## 7. Go-to-market wedge

1. A life captured in art — milestone birthday
2. Honour a lifetime of contribution — retirement
3. The story you built together — anniversary
4. Show them how you see them — gratitude

## 8. Acquisition channels to validate

Prioritise:

- founder network and controlled Swiss beta;
- occasion-based paid search and Meta creatives;
- short reveal videos and recipient reactions with explicit consent;
- partnerships with photographers, retirement planners, wedding suppliers and premium gift shops;
- referral after delivery.

Do not build a complex referral system in MVP. A trackable referral link and manual reward are sufficient.

## 9. Commercial validation thresholds

After at least 100 qualified landing-page visits and at least 20 completed Meaning Maps, evaluate:

| Funnel metric | Minimum signal | Strong signal |
|---|---:|---:|
| Landing → session start | 12% | 20% |
| Session start → Meaning Map | 45% | 60% |
| Meaning Map → preview | 70% | 85% |
| Preview → paid | 6% | 10% |
| Paid → final approval | 85% | 95% |
| Digital → Signature mix | 20% | 35% |
| Voluntary share/referral | 10% | 20% |
| Refund/chargeback | < 5% | < 2% |
| Customer satisfaction | ≥ 4.2/5 | ≥ 4.6/5 |

These thresholds are hypotheses, not promises. The dashboard must expose the actual numbers.

## 10. Strategic moat

The long-term moat is not a model API. It is:

- a proprietary structured Meaning Map;
- outcome data linking inputs, art directions, revisions and satisfaction;
- a recognisable house-style system;
- trusted privacy and consent handling;
- consistent production quality;
- an emotional reveal and referral loop;
- later B2B occasion templates built on the same core engine.
